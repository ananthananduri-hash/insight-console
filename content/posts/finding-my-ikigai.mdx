I didn't set out to build a blog. I set out to pass a Google Analytics certification, and a few weeks in I noticed something: I was taking notes I actually wanted to keep writing past the point the exam required. That's a strange enough feeling that it's worth paying attention to, so I did. Those notes eventually turned into [the GA4 setup guide I wish I'd had on day one](/blog/ga4-for-beginners-setting-up-your-first-property).

## The test I used instead of a mission statement

I'd come across *ikigai* — the Japanese idea that a reason for being sits at the overlap of four things: what you love, what you're good at, what the world needs, what you can get paid for — a while before this. I always thought it was a tidy framework for a LinkedIn post, not something I'd actually use to make a decision.

Then I had an actual decision to make: keep the GA4 notes in a private doc, or build something public with them. I ran it through the four questions almost as a tiebreaker, and the overlap turned out to be larger than I expected:

- **What I love** — turns out it's still writing, specifically. Not "content," writing.
- **What I'm good at, or building toward** — analytics and the marketing side of it, which I was already proving out by literally building the thing.
- **What the world needs** — a less generic, less SEO-farmed explanation of how GA4, SEO, and AEO actually fit together, written by someone doing it in public instead of someone selling a course about it.
- **What I can get paid for** — this is the part that's still in progress. The blog exists to support a job search in marketing analytics, not around it.

Three out of four lined up immediately. The fourth is the one I'm actively testing, which is probably the most honest way to describe a job search anyway.

## Why the blog looks the way it does

Once the four questions overlapped, the shape of the site stopped being a design decision and started being an answer key. The three pillars — analytics, career, healthcare — aren't topics I picked because they sounded broad enough to rank for. Analytics is the "good at" pillar. Career is the "paid for" pillar, written honestly instead of as a highlight reel. Healthcare analytics is the "world needs" pillar — a niche genuinely underserved by generic marketing content, and one I find more interesting the deeper I go.

The "love" pillar isn't a category in the nav. It's the fact that none of this reads like it was outsourced, because it wasn't.

## What I'd tell someone stuck at three overlapping questions instead of four

You don't need all four to line up before you start. I started with three and a bet on the fourth. The bet doesn't resolve by thinking about it longer — it resolves by publishing, watching what happens, and adjusting. That's the whole experiment this blog is running, in public, with real traffic numbers instead of hypothetical ones — including [the technical SEO and AEO decisions behind the site itself](/blog/technical-seo-lessons-from-building-this-with-claude-code).
