A few years ago I wrote a short piece about *ikigai* — the Japanese concept that roughly translates to "a reason for being." At the time I was writing from the outside looking in: researching a concept, admiring a culture, explaining a framework. I didn't yet know I'd eventually need it myself.

## The four questions

Ikigai sits at the intersection of four questions:

1. What do you love?
2. What are you good at?
3. What can you get paid for?
4. What does the world need?

Most career advice optimizes for one or two of these — usually "what can you get paid for" and "what are you good at." It's easy to end up technically successful and quietly unfulfilled, because the other two questions never got asked.

## Where I landed

Writing has been the thread running through my own answer. I put it aside for a long stretch while focused on work — the way most of us do when a job demands full attention. Getting my Google Analytics certification was the trigger to pick it back up, but not because I suddenly loved spreadsheets. It's because analytics gave me something worth writing about that also happens to be useful to my career: a skill I'm building in public, documented as I build it.

That's the actual thesis of this blog. It isn't a generic content farm chasing traffic — it's the place where "what I love doing" (writing), "what I'm good at or building toward" (analytics and marketing), "what I can get paid for" (the job search this supports), and "what the world needs" (clear, honest, non-generic guidance on GA4 and SEO) actually overlap.

## The takeaway, if you're stuck too

If you're in a season where work has crowded out whatever you used to do just because you loved it, you don't need a dramatic life change to get it back. You need one small, deliberate step — and permission to let it look unpolished at first. This post, and this blog, is mine.
